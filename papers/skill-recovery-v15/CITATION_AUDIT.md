## Important corrections and novelty boundaries

1. **CaMeL already preserves state across repairs.** Section 5.4 retains interpreter variables, dependencies and capabilities after exceptions and instructs the planner to account for completed side effects. The same section explicitly excludes atomicity and rollback of failed operations with effects. Do not present state-preserving continuation as a new general idea. Current-value certification and unresolved-effect tests are narrower experimental questions.
2. **Progent already supports continuation after a denial.** Section 4.1 supplies fallback feedback to guide another action and continue the user's task. Section 5 generates and updates policies, while Section 6 checks unapproved expansions. Do not describe prior defenses as simply blocking and abandoning the task, or claim policy generation as the gap.
3. **SkillScope already modifies Skills to preserve utility.** Sections 2.2 and 3 use action removal/replay for necessity checks and control-flow constraints to repair instructions and scripts. Neither graph analysis nor utility-preserving removal is claimed as novel here.
4. **LIFTY entry corrected and strengthened.** The v14 entry used the 2016 title and four authors with an unversioned arXiv URL. That URL now resolves to the expanded six-author 2020 paper. The key `lifty` now cites the peer-reviewed PACMPL/ICFP 2020 publication, with its title, authors, article number and DOI verified on the author's final PDF. The older work remains identifiable specifically as arXiv:1607.03445v1, but is not a second bibliography item.
5. **PASE and MemTX are real, relevant adjacent preprints.** Their primary records and full texts were available. PASE concerns cloud fault recovery with simulated plan assessment, and MemTX concerns memory admission, action gating and cascading repair. Neither establishes an experimental comparison with this manuscript. Do not repeat their reported gains, count them as peer-reviewed publications, or equate simulation/testing with a general formal guarantee.
6. **The two additions address concrete mechanisms.** `rifl` supplies established systems context for completion records and duplicate effects. `metamorphic` supplies established testing context for meaningful input transformations. The synthetic receipt layer does not inherit RIFL's distributed guarantees, and challenge tests do not become proofs by being called metamorphic.

## What the manuscript can claim

- A bounded empirical investigation of how state support, source preservation and finite validation interact after an untrusted Skill is stopped under fixed grants.
- Comparisons against implementations in this study, including shared-state controls and the deterministic compiler; no comparative superiority over named external systems.
- Recorded completion and safety outcomes on the specified synthetic cases, including negative results and failed accepted programs.
- Counterexamples to claims made by an evaluated candidate, not a general correctness or noninterference theorem.

Avoid statements that all prior defenses terminate blocked tasks, that secure repair or state preservation is new, that tested receipts provide production exactly-once delivery, or that the absence of observed leakage proves general safety. The new references strengthen attribution; they do not expand the evidence supplied by the experiments.
